# Project

A day planner for college students on Canvas. It turns their assignments into a plan that fits their body clock, then helps them focus through it. Not a todo app, a chatbot, or a Canvas replacement. Hackathon theme: "modern life and/or nature life".

Devices: laptop browser.

## How we build it

It began as a hackathon presentation. On 2026-10-16 the owner moved it to full-effort development: the standards in `~/.claude/CLAUDE.md` apply in full. Where this file and that one differ, this file wins.

- **Models** (the owner's calls): Sonnet 5.5 (`claude-sonnet-5-5`) at medium effort for every call that thinks: reading assignments, What's wrong, the plan, the advice and the nature suggestions. Haiku 5.5 (`claude-haiku-5-5`) with no thinking only for Lockdown's site check, which a page load waits on. **Reschedule → Week** runs on an open model through OpenRouter, `qwen/qwen3.8-27b` pinned to the provider `modelrun/fp4`, with no reasoning (the owner's call: "I don't wanna wait 40s on stage"; `src/lib/openrouter.ts`, key `OPENROUTER_API_KEY`, server-only): same prompt and answer shape, about 1–5 seconds and a third of a cent. If it fails or there's no key, the plan falls back to Sonnet; if it answers with no sessions at all, it's asked once more, and then code places the work. No comparing models or effort levels. **Short answers:** a model answers with numbers and short keys wherever it can (the plan's sessions are `s: [{t, d, at, m}]`: task number, day number, start, minutes), since every token is one it has to write.
- **Mature libraries over hand-rolled UI** (the owner's call): Radix Themes for controls and floating cards, FullCalendar for the week grid, styled from one palette.
- **Sign-in is an email, nothing more** (the owner's call): typing an email identifies the student, new or returning, with no password. Anyone who types an email gets that planner, which is fine for demos and not for real students. **Online, a passphrase keeps it to the hackathon** (the owner's call): the login also asks for it, and `/api/login` checks it against `ACCESS_CODE` (server-only, set in Vercel, never in the repo; a constant-time compare). A deployed app without `ACCESS_CODE` lets nobody in; a local dev server without one is open. Wrong or missing: "Wrong passphrase."

## Words

- **assignment**: what Canvas gives us.
- **task**: an assignment after import, with its interpretation.
- **import**: a Canvas calendar feed → tasks.
- **feed**: one school's Canvas, named by its address ("deanza.instructure.com"). A student can have several, each from a feed link or an uploaded `.ics` file.
- **interpretation**: what the model reads from one assignment: the deliverable (what to hand in), requirements, minutes (the time estimate) and work (`deep` or `light`). "Focus" is kept for focus mode.
- **body clock**: when the student sleeps, their **chronotype** (early, middle or late type) and their **alertness** hour by hour, with the **sharpest** hours marked. Learned from browser history by the optional extension ("usage pattern learning"), or set as a **morning person** or **night person**; until then a default of 11pm to 8am.
- **active hours**: the share of recent days the browser was used in each hour. Only the extension popup's bars show it now; planning uses the body clock.
- **extension**: the Chrome extension. Optional: the app never requires or asks for it. When connected it unlocks usage pattern learning and **Lockdown**.
- **lockdown**: with Lockdown on in Options (its only switch, off by default), distracting sites are blocked whenever a session is under way by the plan, or while focus mode runs on any task (working ahead of schedule counts). Haiku judges each site once (study, distraction or unsure); only distractions are blocked. Needs the extension.
- **block**: anything on the calendar: a session, a break, an event, or a nature suggestion.
- **session**: a stretch of time on one task, with a start and minutes.
- **event**: the student's own block, added with "+" (a shift at work, a club meeting).
- **class**: a weekly meeting from the student's class schedule (a lecture, lab or discussion), read from a screenshot or PDF they upload in Options. Fixed, like an event, and shown like one.
- **week**: the school week, Sunday to Saturday ("that's how school works"). A plan covers this week and the next: today through Saturday of next week, so there's always a full week ahead to show (the owner's call for the demo).
- **reschedule**: the one way to (re)plan: it asks "What's wrong?" and how much may move (**scope**), then plans. ("Rearrange" is retired.)
- **preferences**: how the student likes to work, set in Options, each a pair of opposites with Any between, the choices saying what they mean: **Chunking** 25–45 min / 60–90 min, **Start with** Easy work / Hard work, **Breaks** 10 min / 30 min, **Courses a day** One or two / Several, **Finish** Early / Near due, **Weekends** Lighter / Full. They steer the planner over the research guidelines, never over the hard rules.
- **scope**: what may move. **Day**: one day's work: today's remaining work, or another day the plan covers (the day on screen in Day, a day picked from the shown week in Week, the session's own day from its card). **Week**: everything from now on, planned through next Saturday (and any session left further out from an older plan). A busy time or lighter day from the answer also moves the work it touches. ("Hour" was removed, the owner's call: too buggy.)
- **lighter day**: a day the answer asked to carry less ("I feel sick today"): at most so many minutes, and no deep work unless it says so. Shown as a chip on Day; deletable.
- **biggest priority**: shown with every Reschedule result: the open tasks due soonest, then the most work left. Plain code.
- **reschedule from a session**: a session card's Reschedule. That session moves and its task waits an hour, plus whatever the scope opens up. ("Dismiss" is retired.)
- **what's wrong**: the question Reschedule asks first. The answer can become a lighter day, a **busy time** (one of the student's events, titled in their words: "Brother's wedding"), or a **break** now (nap, walk, eat or move). An empty or vague answer changes nothing.
- **nature suggestion**: one outing a day to a real park, garden, reserve or trail near the student, on top of the plan, with the walk there and back. ("Free time" ideas are retired.)
- **due**: an assignment shown on the day it's due ("assignment block" in the sketches).
- **unplanned**: open work the plan hasn't placed: a task due by the plan's last day whose sessions don't cover its minutes yet (it didn't fit, or it's still being planned). Shown in Week with what's left.
- **focus mode**: one session, full screen, in pomodoros of the student's lengths. Entered only from a session's card (focus is per task).

## The app

One Next.js 16 app (App Router, Bun, Biome, Vitest) with Supabase.

- **Data:** Supabase Postgres, schema in `supabase/migrations/`. Tables: `settings` (time zone, active hours, nights, morning or night person, preferences, Lockdown and the pomodoro's lengths, nearby places), `feeds` (one per school, with when it was imported and when its work was last planned), `tasks` (each under its feed), `blocks` (session / break / event / nature, a nature one carrying its place), `day_limits` (lighter days), `classes` (weekly meetings: days, start, minutes, room, the term's dates), `dismissals` (a task rescheduled from a session waits an hour). Reminders were removed (the owner's call). Every row belongs to its owner under RLS.
- **Sign-in:** `/login` asks for an email; `/api/login` starts the session (`src/app/api/login/route.ts`). It asks Supabase's admin API for a one-time sign-in token for that email (creating the account the first time; nothing is sent) and verifies it at once as the student, which sets the session cookie. The secret key (`SUPABASE_SECRET_KEY`, server-only, `src/lib/supabase/admin.ts`) is used for that and nothing else; all data is still read and written as the student under RLS. The proxy (`src/proxy.ts` → `src/lib/supabase/proxy.ts`) sends signed-out visitors to `/login?next=…` and answers API calls with 401. Each page also checks the user with the auth server (`requireUser`), which catches a session whose account is gone. A 401 in the app clears the session and goes to `/login`. Sign out is in Options. Anonymous sign-in is off.
- **Session cookie:** the app's own name, `sb-planner-auth-token` (`src/lib/supabase/env.ts`). Renna's local Supabase is also on 127.0.0.1 and browsers share localhost cookies across ports, so the default name let the two apps overwrite each other's sign-in.
- **Online:** https://dahacksproj.vercel.app (Vercel project `planner` in nothflares-projects; Supabase project `planner`, ref `zvjthlrobyeykrmkpwna`, US West, free plan). Deploy with `bunx vercel deploy --prod` (the Vercel CLI isn't installed globally); `.vercelignore` keeps keys, the Python reference, scripts, test output and `supabase/` out of the upload (entries anchored to the root: a bare `supabase/` also drops `src/lib/supabase`). Production settings live in Vercel: the two public Supabase values, `SUPABASE_SECRET_KEY` (fetched with `supabase projects api-keys --reveal`; without `--reveal` the CLI hands out a masked copy), `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`, `OPENROUTER_API_KEY` and `ACCESS_CODE`. Schema changes: `supabase db push` (the project is linked; its database password is `SUPABASE_DB_PASSWORD` in `.env.development.local`). Online the server runs on UTC, so a mock link is read in the student's time zone (the import adds `?tz=`). The walks after an import are asked for by whatever page is open when its plan finishes (`WalksAfterPlanning` in `App.tsx`), so a reload mid-import still gets them.
- **Local dev:** Supabase runs in OrbStack on its own ports (API 54421, DB 54422, Studio 54423) so it sits beside Renna's. `supabase start`, then `bun run dev`. Keys for dev live in `.env.development.local` (not committed): `NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_URL`, `NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_PUBLISHABLE_KEY`, `SUPABASE_SECRET_KEY`, `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`, `OPENROUTER_API_KEY`. Every model call logs its tokens and cost in the server log (`tally` in `src/lib/claude.ts`; `openrouter …` lines for the fast model). After a schema change: `supabase db reset` and `bun run db:types` (it reads the local database by its address: since the cloud project was linked, `--local` sends the cloud password and fails); online, `supabase db push`.
- **Reading and plain writes:** the page loads everything once (`fetchPlanner`, `src/modules/planner/core/queries.ts`) into one TanStack Query cache. Done ticks, events, preferences, focus settings and what the extension learned are written straight from the browser through RLS, optimistic and in order (`src/modules/planner/ui/planner.tsx`).
- **Model calls and outside services** go through route handlers that check the body and the user: `/api/import`, `/api/reschedule` and `/api/reschedule/undo`, `/api/places` (finds places, then plans every day's nature suggestion), `/api/nature` (plans the walks on the days given, or every day: after a reschedule, an import or a hand-set time; it first waits for an import's plan, `untilPlanned`), and for the extension `/api/site` (Lockdown's site check), `/api/status` (who's signed in) and `/api/lockdown` (the setting and the session under way). The API key is server-only (`src/lib/claude.ts`). A network failure reads "Couldn't reach Planner. Try again."; a 401 clears the session and goes to `/login`.
- **Time:** code owns it, in the student's time zone, with Temporal (`src/lib/time.ts`). The model never does date math; it picks a day number and an HH:MM, and code checks it.
- **Demo clock:** `?at=HH:MM` on any page pins "now" for today, and Reschedule uses it too. Only `?at=` travels between screens (`useKeep`); Day and Week each keep their own `?date=`, so switching views opens today.
- **Modules:** `src/modules/<feature>/core` holds plain functions (`import`, `arrange`, `suggest`, `planner`); `src/modules/planner/ui` and `src/modules/extension/ui` hold client hooks; screens are in `src/shell/`.
- **Checks:** `bun run lint && bun run typecheck && bun run test && bun run build`.
- **Mock schools (for demos):** the owner's real feed doesn't show every feature, so the demo runs on two made-up schools. `/mock/state.ics` (Mock State University: 23 entries over five courses, PHYS, ENGL, PSYC, BIOL, MATH) has three things due today including a timed quiz tonight, long work that must spread over days (an essay, two lab reports), quizzes, an exam to prepare for, discussion replies, two title-only locked items, and a past assignment and office hours that must stay out. `/mock/college.ics` (Mock Community College: 6 entries) is the second school, sharing a Canvas id with Mock State. Dates are relative to today (`?tz=` sets the zone), written the way Canvas writes them (`src/modules/import/core/mock.ts`). For the presentation everything upcoming falls in this calendar week and the next, the span a plan covers, and timed quizzes are due in the evening so a demo at any hour keeps them: the entries are written over two weeks from a Sunday; today's three stay today, and the rest are spread evenly, in order, over tomorrow through next Saturday, so whatever the day there are three things due today and at most four on any day. Each school also lists what was due earlier this week (`earlier`), in the feed once it's past, as in Canvas. Because they're demo data (`isMockSchool`), importing one fills in this week so far, so the Week view is full from Sunday (see Import). Paste either link into Options; for Classes, upload `/mock/schedule.png`.
- **In the browser:** `bun scripts/e2e-ui.ts` drives Chrome (Playwright, the installed Chrome) through every screen as a new student with a faked extension (active hours and a late sleeper's nights), then as a second student with none (the default body clock, planning still works). It checks, among the rest: no Reminders, no sidebar Focus, Lockdown only in Options, the body clock sentence, at most one nature suggestion a day and its card (map, walk, Directions on foot). Screenshots and the full console log go to `UI_OUT` (default `.e2e-ui/`, not committed). Blocks carry `data-kind` (session, break, event, nature, due) for it. A hydration note about `caret-color` on the login field comes from Playwright hiding the text cursor for screenshots, not from the app. The extension itself is tested in Playwright's bundled Chromium (installed Chrome refuses to load an unpacked extension that way).
- **End-to-end:** `bun run e2e` (`scripts/e2e.ts`), with Supabase and the dev server running. It plays real visitors (signing in by email) against the mock schools: the sign-in gate, import by link and upload, two schools, planning the week, Day on another day (only that day's work moves, everything else stays put), "I feel sick today" (a light day the plan keeps to), a wedding tomorrow (a busy block, work moved around it), Undo (everything back exactly), rescheduling from a session ("starving" → a break to eat; "ugh" → nothing), a new shift moving only the session it covers, the hard rules after every change, a done tick, removing a school, the pages, and privacy between students. It works out what each mock school holds from the feed as it parses at that moment (the entries are spread over the days left, so the counts change with the day), and, with Sonnet doing the thinking, costs well under a dollar a run.

## Import

Each upcoming assignment becomes a task: title, course, due time, a link to the assignment page, and its interpretation. Pipeline: a feed link or an uploaded `.ics` → read assignments → file them under their school → only new or changed ones go to Claude → check every field → save. Lives in `src/modules/import/core/`: `feed.ts` (read the feed, with ical.js), `interpret.ts` (prompt and schema), `reading.ts` (checks), `run.ts` (the pipeline).

- Feed link, not a Canvas login key: every student has one, and the school doesn't need to approve anything. Uploading the `.ics` works too, but it doesn't update itself.
- Several schools: a feed is named by the Canvas address in its assignment links. A link and a file from the same school land in the same feed, so the same assignments never appear twice. Canvas ids are unique within one school, so a task's key is (feed, uid). Removing a school removes its tasks and their sessions.
- Canvas writes an assignment due at 11:59pm as an all-day date. Code sets it back to 11:59pm in the student's time zone.
- Only assignments come in (UIDs `event-…assignment…-<id>`). Class meetings and personal events are skipped, and so is anything already past due, except from a mock school.
- **A mock school brings this week so far** (demo data only, `isMockSchool`): what it had due since Sunday comes in too, ticked done, and is read like the rest. Then, before the plan, its work and today's is laid into Sunday through now as done sessions by the planner's own rules (`fillWeekSoFar`, `weekSoFar` in `src/modules/arrange/core/week-so-far.ts`): each task in the two days before it was due, today's today, never past now; today's work fully done by now is ticked. Only tasks with no sessions yet, so a second import changes nothing. A real school's week starts at its import.
- Assignments in locked modules have no text in the feed, so they're interpreted from the title alone.
- Claude Sonnet 5.5 at medium effort, with a fixed output schema (zod). The model never sees dates. Code drops fields that don't hold: minutes outside 5 to 2,400, empty lines or lines over 160 characters, more than 5 requirements. Gaps stay empty and are never guessed.
- **A due date that moved:** on import, sessions that would now end after their task is due are removed, and the next Reschedule places that work again.
- Each assignment is read once per version of its text (fingerprint of course + title + text). An extension alone doesn't cost a model call. A failed read is retried on the next import.
- Reading happens in the background: `/api/import` saves the tasks and answers at once ("15 upcoming"), then `after()` reads the new or changed ones (`readAssignments`) and saves each reading the moment it lands. Up to 32 model calls run at once in a steady pool (`p-limit`); the client retries up to 4 times to ride out rate limits. 78 assignments take about 9 seconds.
- **The work plans itself:** once an import is read, the same `after()` runs Reschedule with scope "fill" (no press needed; a mock school's week so far first): the work not planned yet is placed around everything already planned, and the school's `planned_at` is set. One automatic plan runs at a time per student (`oneAtATime` in `src/modules/arrange/core/lock.ts`, a turn taken in `settings.planning_since`), so two schools imported together plan one after the other. While a recent import (5 minutes, `PLANNING_WINDOW_MS`) is newer than its `planned_at` (`isPlanning`), the page checks back every 2 seconds, the Day and Week headers show "Planning" with the sweep, and the Reschedule button waits. After that, while places nearby are looked up or a day's walk is planned again, they show "Finding walks" the same way (`natureKey`).
- While a just-imported school (within 3 minutes, `READING_WINDOW_MS`) has unread tasks, the page checks back every second. Its row in Options shows "Reading n of N" with a growing bar and a quiet sweep, the Day and Week headers show the total the same way (`Reading.tsx`, `ReadingAll.tsx`), and unread Due chips pulse slowly. Motion tokens live in `globals.css`; with reduced motion the sweep and pulse stop.

## Body clock

When the student sleeps, their chronotype, and how alert they are through the day. Plain code, no model: `src/modules/arrange/core/body-clock.ts` (`bodyClockOf`, `describe`). Pipeline: browser history → each night's longest gap in browsing → usual sleep and wake times → middle of sleep on free days = chronotype → an alertness curve hour by hour → the planner.

- **Nights** (`sleepTimes` in `extension/activity.js`): for each of the last 28 mornings, the longest gap with no visits between 6pm the day before and 2pm, when it's at least 3 hours with a visit on both sides. Its start stands for falling asleep and its end for getting up (the first visit comes a little after waking, so it runs late; good enough for the demo). Saved in `settings.nights`, as minutes from that morning's midnight.
- **Learned** from at least 5 nights: the median sleep and wake times. **Chronotype** is the median middle of sleep on Saturday and Sunday mornings (all nights if fewer than two), as in the Munich Chronotype Questionnaire: before 3:30am early, after 5am late, else middle.
- **Set by the student:** Morning (10pm to 6am) or Night (2am to 10am), in `settings.clock`; it wins over what was learned. **Default** without either: 11pm to 8am.
- **Alertness:** the Three-Process Model of Alertness (Åkerstedt & Folkard). Sleep pressure builds from waking, a 24-hour rhythm peaks in the afternoon and shifts with chronotype, and grogginess fades in the first hour. It's scaled to 10–100 for awake hours, 0 asleep. **Sharpest** is the stretch around the peak within 5% of it.
- **Shown** in Options: "Up around 9:30am, asleep around 1:30am · Late type · Sharpest 2pm–8pm", the 24 bars with the sharpest in ink, where it comes from ("From your browser, 14 nights", "Set by you", "Default"), and Morning · Any · Night.
- **Used** by the planner in place of the old active hours: the prompt gets the sentence and the 24 values, deep work goes to the most alert hours, and `check` never places a session in an hour marked 0. What's wrong gets this hour's alertness.

The extension is optional: nothing in the app requires or asks for it. The app checks once per page load with a `ping` (`extensionPresent`, `useExtension` in `bridge.ts`), and on every load asks for what it learned (`activityFromExtension`), saving the active hours and nights when they change. Only this device's visits count, read from history on demand with no background tracking. Install: chrome://extensions → Developer mode → Load unpacked → `extension/`. The content script matches the live address and `localhost:3000` / `127.0.0.1:3000`; until the app has been opened, the extension talks to the live one.

## Reschedule

Pipeline: "What's wrong?" → Sonnet (medium effort) turns the answer into changes in a fixed schema → `checkChanges` keeps what holds → saved (a lighter day in `day_limits`, a busy time as an event block, a break) → the scope picks which sessions may move → one Sonnet call (medium effort) places them, as task number + day number + HH:MM + minutes (short keys: `t`, `d`, `at`, `m`) → `check` drops every session that breaks a hard rule (the reasons are logged) → **code fills the gaps** the model left: the work still unplaced goes, soonest due first, into free slots that meet the same rules with a 10-minute break either side (from its own sessions and from anything that stays: sessions, classes, other plans), deep work at the most alert hours and light work at lower ones, away from the same task → **deep work to the peak:** within each day a deep and a light session trade times when that puts the deep work at a sharper hour and no break or variety is lost (`checkAndFill`, `deepAtPeak`, `crowding` in `plan.ts`) → the moved sessions are replaced. Lives in `src/modules/arrange/core/`: `plan.ts` (the week, the rules, `check`, the prompt text) and `arrange.ts` (`reschedule`, `undoReschedule`); the answer step in `src/modules/suggest/core/` (`understand.ts`, `changes.ts`, `rest.ts`). Reschedule → Week takes 1–5 seconds on the fast model (20–40 on Sonnet); Day a few seconds; the plan after an import, on Sonnet, 40–80 seconds in the background.

- **The plan's days:** today (from the next quarter hour) through Saturday of next week (`weekFrom`). Only tasks due by that Saturday are planned, so the plan and Unplanned agree; the next week's work comes in when its week does (each Sunday the plan reaches a week further), shown as Unplanned until a Reschedule or import places it. The result's "didn't fit" counts the minutes still owed to tasks due by then.
- **Scopes** (what may move): Classes (automatic, after a class schedule upload): just the sessions a class now covers. Day: today's remaining sessions, including one under way, or all of the chosen day's, placed on that day where it allows, else on other days before they're due. Week: every future session this week. Clash (automatic, after adding an event): just the sessions under it. Whatever the scope, a busy time or lighter day from the answer also moves the sessions it touches. With Day and Clash each task may place only the minutes that moved (`allowance`); other sessions are busy times that stay put. A session the student rescheduled from always moves, and its task waits an hour. Fill (automatic, after an import is read): nothing moves; each task places only the minutes its sessions don't cover yet.
- **What the model sees:** each task's title, course, one-line deliverable, due time, minutes to place and deep/light; each day's open time, busy times and lighter-day limit; the body clock (the sentence and alertness by hour); the student's preferences; the plan before (to keep steady); and the scope's instruction, last.
- **Preferences** (`src/modules/arrange/core/preferences.ts`): a registry of pairs, each choice one line of the planner's prompt ("Long sessions: 60 to 90 minutes each, fewer of them."). The system prompt ranks them above the research guidelines and below the hard rules; they aren't checked in code, since they're wishes. Saved in `settings.preferences`; a new preference is one more entry in the registry. Tried on one week: Long gave 8 sessions with a median of 60 minutes, Short 17 with a median of 30.
- **Undo:** every reschedule returns exactly what it changed (sessions removed, blocks and limits added, a limit it replaced). `/api/reschedule/undo` puts it back, as the student under RLS. Undo also takes back the hour-long wait a session's Reschedule set. A reschedule that fails midway takes back what it had already saved (lighter day, busy time, break, wait), so nothing is left half done.

The prompt carries only time-based research. Retrieval practice (self-testing) is deliberately left out to keep the purpose clear.
- **Deadlines first.**
- **Synchrony effect:** deep work in the student's most alert hours, light work in lower ones (from the body clock).
- **Spacing:** work over 90 minutes is split across the days before it's due: studied material sticks better spread over days (the spacing effect), and coming back to an essay after a night's sleep helps.
- **Vary the work:** after a session the next one is usually a different task, ideally another course, and never more than two in a row on the same task. Focus wears down over hours on one task, and mixing subjects helps learning (interleaving). This replaced "one course at a time": task-switching costs are about switching every few seconds, not between hour-long sessions, and the old rule produced long runs of one task.
- **Breaks:** sessions of 25–90 minutes, at least 10 minutes apart.
- **Pacing:** attention wears down with time on task, and a real break restores it: sessions less than 30 minutes apart are one stretch of work, and a stretch holds at most 90 minutes (`STRETCH`, `REST`, `stretch` in `plan.ts`). Added after a plan packed 4 hours of deep work into the sharpest hours with 10-minute gaps, every day.
- **Planning fallacy:** a third of each day's open time stays free, and deep work is capped at 4 hours a day.
- **Fixed busy times:** never overlapped.
- **A lighter day:** keeps within its minutes, and gets no deep work unless it allows it.
- **Steadiness:** place only what's moving, and keep the plan before where possible. The student's preferences outrank steadiness (otherwise a Week plan would never change shape for them).

**Measured** (`bun scripts/principles.ts`, a late sleeper with both mock schools, one run each, about 25 cents): deep work's average alertness 97 of 100 against light work's 82; every long task spread over two or more days; no back-to-back sessions under 10 minutes; the same task twice in a row 3 times (forced by work due that night; 9–10 before "Vary the work"), and 24 of 27 neighbours changing course (13 of 24 before). No deep work in the hour before sleep, so no rule for it: a rule that changes nothing stays out. With pacing (one run, Friday night, the plan on import and a Week reschedule): no stretch of work over 90 minutes (8 of 13 before, the longest 4 hours), deep work's alertness still 97 against light's 86–88. Late in a week the mock schools hold more deep work than the 4-hour daily cap leaves room for, so some doesn't fit (800 minutes on that Friday); every day is at the cap, so pacing isn't what leaves it out. Days look alike because the body clock is the same every day: the hard work starts at the same sharp hour.

Code checks (`check`): the task exists, the day is this week, the time parses, the session is 10–90 minutes long, its stretch of work (sessions under 30 minutes apart) at most 90 minutes, inside that day's window, after any wait, ends before the task is due, never while asleep (alertness 0), doesn't overlap a busy time or another session, stays within the task's minutes (or what moved), the day's 4-hour deep cap, its lighter-day limit, and its free third.

## Classes

The student's class schedule, so the plan keeps clear of class. Pipeline: Options → Upload a screenshot or PDF of the schedule from the school's portal → `/api/classes` → Sonnet 5.5 at medium effort reads the weekly meetings into a fixed schema (title, weekdays as numbers, start and end as shown, room, the term's first and last days only if shown; online or asynchronous classes, exams and holidays left out; no date math) → `checkClasses` keeps what holds (weekdays 0–6, times that parse, 10–360 minutes, short title and room, a term that runs forwards; at most 30) → the ones not saved already are added (a student at two schools uploads two schedules; the same one twice adds nothing) → Reschedule with scope "classes" moves the planned work a class now covers, and the walks on those days are planned again. Lives in `src/modules/classes/core/` (`read.ts` the model, `reading.ts` the checks, `classes.ts` the weekly rules → times) and `src/app/api/classes/route.ts`.

- **Weekly rules, not copies:** `classesOn` / `classTimes` turn a rule into its meetings on any day in the student's time zone, within the term when the schedule shows one. Day and Week show them as gray blocks like events (`kind: "class"` items; the card shows the title, time and room; changed in Options, not on the card). The planner (`arrange.ts`, busy for every scope), the walks (`nature-run.ts`), the filled-in week so far and hand-set times (`conflictOf`: "Overlaps PSYC 1-02 Lecture, 1:30–2:45pm") all keep clear of them.
- **Sample for the demo:** `/mock/schedule.png`, Mock State's schedule as a portal would show it: seven weekly meetings over the five mock courses, the term's dates, and an online class that must be left out.
- **Tried:** the sample read exactly (7 of 7 meetings, the online one left out, rooms and term dates right) for about a cent; 10 planned sessions moved and none overlap a class; 31 s for the whole upload (reading, then the replan on Sonnet).

## What's wrong

Lives in `src/modules/suggest/core/`.

What's wrong (`understand.ts`, `changes.ts`, `rest.ts`): the answer, the time, this hour's alertness, the nap cutoff, minutes worked today, the last break, the session it came from, the plan's days and, for Day on another day, which day (what the answer describes is on that day unless it names one) go to Sonnet (medium effort). It returns lighter days (day number, minutes, deep allowed), busy times (day number, HH:MM to HH:MM, a short title in the student's words) and at most one break. `checkChanges` keeps days inside the week, real times still ahead, titles under 60 characters, minutes 0–720, and holds the break to its kind's range. Code writes the words shown ("Today: at most 1h of work, nothing heavy."). The break starts now, or after a session under way when the whole week is planned. Breaks:
- **nap**, 10–20 min: sleepy or drained. Longer naps leave you groggy, and there's no nap within 7 hours of when the student usually falls asleep (`lastNap` in `rest.ts`, from the body clock: 4pm for an 11pm sleeper, 6:30pm for a 1:30am one), because a late nap takes away the sleep pressure that brings sleep on.
- **walk**, 10–30 min: can't focus, restless, stressed. Attention Restoration Theory: time outside restores focus.
- **eat**, 15–40 min: hungry.
- **move**, 5–15 min: stiff, bored, sitting too long.
- **nothing:** vague or empty answers change nothing; the plan just runs in its scope.

**Advice** (`advice.ts`, `advise.ts`): beside the reschedule, Sonnet 5.5 (medium effort) writes one practical line for this person at this hour, pointing to an option they may have forgotten: sleep now, eat a real meal, the campus health center, emailing the professor or TA for an extension, office hours. It gets the weekday and time, the estimated alertness this hour, the nap cutoff, the body clock in words, minutes worked today, the session it came from and the rest of today; the answer comes last. It never diagnoses, cheers or says the plan moved; what the student says they feel comes before the estimate, which it never quotes, and past the nap cutoff it suggests something else (a walk outside, bright light, an early night). Anything that sounds like an emergency points to the local emergency number, or 988 in the US for a crisis. The prompt asks for at most 18 words; code keeps one trimmed line of at most 140 characters and 24 words, else nothing. An empty answer makes no call, and a vague one gets an empty line, so nothing shows. `/api/reschedule` runs it in parallel with `reschedule()`, so it adds no wait; a failure only means no advice. It isn't saved: it shows as the first line of the Reschedule result. Tried live: "i am sick" at 1am → "Go to sleep now, and if you still feel sick in the morning, call the campus health center."

## Nature suggestions

One outing a day to a real place near the student, on top of the plan. Pipeline: an import (Add or Upload) and Reschedule ask the browser where the student is (the browser asks permission the first time; `useNearbyPlaces`) → when nothing is saved yet, or they've moved more than 3 km, places are found in the background (`/api/places`): OpenStreetMap's Overpass API (free, no key; named parks, reserves, gardens and hiking routes within 5 km, not sports grounds; four public servers asked at once, the first full answer winning, since each is often slow or failing (tried: 24–42 s for the main one, 7–16 s for a mirror, errors from the rest; one at a time took 38–51 s); what an area of about a kilometre holds is kept on the server for a day, so another search there skips this; the 12 kept are the nearest, with four kept for the nearest trails, reserves and gardens so a light day can get a bigger place) → each place's walk from there (FOSSGIS foot routing, `walk.ts`, the server openstreetmap.org itself uses; the public OSRM demo isn't used because it returns car times whatever the profile; a 429 is retried once; when FOSSGIS is down or takes over 4 seconds, straight line × 1.3 at 5 km/h, marked "About") → 12 saved in `settings.places` → for the days that need one, code works out the free gaps (after now, between sessions, events and breaks, never other nature suggestions, while awake) → Sonnet 5.5 at medium effort picks a place, a start and a stay (15–90 min) per day, with closer places or shorter stays on heavy days (`nature.ts`, `nature-pick.ts`) → `checkNature` keeps a pick only if the walk there, the stay and the walk back fit one gap and end by sunset at the place (suncalc) and by 20:30; a day where nothing fits gets none → saved as a `nature` block with its place (`nature-run.ts`, `refreshNature`). Places are looked up when an import or reschedule asks for walks and nothing is saved yet or the student has moved; otherwise the saved ones are used. During an import `/api/places` waits for its plan before planning the walks, so they're placed around the work.

- **Never blocking:** Reschedule doesn't treat nature suggestions as busy, and a new event over one replans that day (scope clash, even with no work under it). A day whose outing has already started keeps it and gets no second one. A failed nature refresh never fails the reschedule. **No plan waits on a walk:** a reschedule places the work, clears the not-yet-started nature suggestions on the days it changed (every day for Week, `clearNature`) and answers; the page then asks for new ones on those days (`useWalks` → `/api/nature`, one Sonnet call, a few seconds), with "Finding walks" in the header meanwhile, and they join that reschedule's Undo (`addWalks`), so Undo still puts the old ones back exactly. Tried: a Week reschedule showed its plan at 4 s and its walks at 7 s (before, the plan waited about 4 s more for the walks). An import asks the same way once it's answered; the request waits for the import's plan, then plans every day. Nature calls take turns (`oneAtATime`), so a day never gets two.
- **The map:** a still picture of the walk (`MapPicture.tsx`, `map-view.ts`): Esri's Dark Gray Canvas basemap tiles (no key) at the closest zoom that holds the route with room around it (16 at most, the basemap's closest), put together into one bitmap at twice the size, dimmed to 60% over the sheet, with the walking route and the two points drawn over it in SVG (Web Mercator, plain code) and the credit as small quiet text in the corner. Tiles rather than Esri's one-image export: tiles are cached by the browser for a day, the export isn't and took 1.5–5.6 s each when tried. It's made in full before it's shown (the route asked for, waiting up to 0.7 s; every tile fetched and decoded, or no picture: a map with a hole would look broken; tiles placed on whole pixels, or their joins show as lines) and drawn from the bitmap onto a canvas before the first frame paints, so once on screen nothing in it moves or arrives late. One size for the preview and the card (308 × 168, the card's width inside its padding), so they share the picture. A picture made without its route is made again, for next time, when the route arrives. Routes come from FOSSGIS on foot, one request per pair, kept for the session (an estimate too). The walks on screen get their pictures made as the page loads, three at a time (one at a time while idle was too slow: a walk hovered soon after loading opened late). The connection to the tile server is opened as the page loads (`preconnect` in `layout.tsx`). Replaced a live Leaflet map (owner: "super unstable"; measured frame by frame: grey tiles, then the route arriving and the map sliding 5–10 px, the first one re-zooming). The walk's card shows the same picture (an empty box of the same size until it's ready, usually already).
- **The preview:** pointing at a nature block shows the picture with one row under it: the place on the left, cut short with an ellipsis, "8 min walk" quiet on the right (after Altay's map location preview). The same flat card with a gray edge as every floating card (`.map-preview` in `globals.css`). It's a Radix Tooltip, so all walks share one timing (`Tooltip.Provider` in `App.tsx`): half a second's rest opens it with a fade; within 300 ms of one closing, the next walk's opens at once with no fade, and a closing one goes at once, so two are never on screen and nothing travels; a click closes it until the pointer comes back, and focus returning from the walk's card doesn't open it; scrolling what holds the walk closes it. Radix alone decides open and closed; the card is only drawn once its picture is ready (or without a map, the name and walk alone, if the picture can't be made). Holding Radix's `open` back until then instead left the two out of step, and a walk the pointer had already left opened when its picture arrived. Nothing in it to point at: the pointer passes through it and through the box Radix positions it in (`[data-radix-popper-content-wrapper]:has(> .map-preview)`) to the walk underneath (in Week it covers the next day's), and leaving the walk closes it; without the box too, a hop onto the next walk landed on the box and that walk never heard the pointer arrive. Measured: every hop between neighbouring days swaps to the next preview within 30 ms; a rest opens one about half a second after arriving. It sits where the walk's card opens: to the right of the block, top-aligned, flipping left at the edge. Directions open openstreetmap.org on foot (`engine=fossgis_osrm_foot`).
- **Altay's map test** (branch `mapIntegration`: `MapTest.html`, `MapTest.js`, a standalone page listing nearby parks and trails with a hover map preview and the route) is where the map preview and walking route came from. It's merged into `main` with its author kept, and lives at the root, outside our formatter. Its routing server (the public OSRM demo) and directions engine (`osrm_foot`) gave car times; both now walk, in his page and in the app.

## Focus mode

`/focus/[id]` (`src/shell/Focus.tsx`): one session in pomodoros of the student's lengths (Options → Focus: Work 25 / 50 / 90 min, Break 5 / 10 / 15; `settings.focus_minutes`, `break_minutes`). A rest left over at the very end is dropped (`pomodoros`). Entered only from a session's card. Focus mode always tells the extension when it starts or resumes (`{ type: "focus", seconds, title, course }`, `seconds` being what's left of the whole session) and when it pauses or ends (`unfocus`); whether that blocks anything is the Lockdown setting in Options. Whole-page visits only are blocked, so the embedded lofi player still plays. Done waits for the save, then tells the extension (`sync`), so Lockdown lifts at once; the Options switch also tells it after saving. Closing the tab doesn't end focus mode (the owner's call: Lockdown can't be shut by closing a tab); only Pause, Done or Leave does, or its time running out. A focus page that opens while focus runs for its session (a reload, or a distraction Lockdown sent there) picks the clock up from the extension (`focus-state`): the pomodoro and the seconds left, counted back from when it ends. The focus message names the session as `block`, not `id`: `id` is the bridge's own reply id, and the two overwrote each other, so the extension never knew which session was in focus.

## The extension

`extension/` (Manifest V3, plain JS, "Planner" 0.9.0). Its pages use the app's look (`ui.css`: the dark tokens from `globals.css`, Geist bundled under its OFL licence in `fonts/`).

- **When:** the extension keeps the focus state the app sends and asks the app every minute, on each app page load, on focus or unfocus, and on `sync` (sent when the Options switch changes) through `GET /api/lockdown`: the setting, and the not-done session under way now (`sessionCovering` in `src/modules/extension/core/lockdown.ts`, read as the student under RLS; `?at=` honoured). It blocks when the setting is on and focus mode runs or a session is under way, until the later of the two ends. The app asks on every page load too (with `activity`), so a session that starts is caught at once. **A blocked visit goes to the app's own focus page** (`toFocus`), for the focus session if focus mode runs, else the session under way (its block id comes with `focus` and from `/api/lockdown`): the blocked tab itself turns into that page (the owner's call: no jump to another tab that happens to be open). Neither needs the app open: the extension keeps focus mode itself and asks `/api/lockdown` every minute in the background. Tried with the extension in Chromium: focus started, its tab closed, YouTube opened → that tab became the session's focus page with the clock running on; after Leave, YouTube opened; with no app tab at all and a session under way, YouTube went to that session's focus page within the minute. When Lockdown starts, tabs already open on a distraction are closed and focus comes forward (`sweep`); sites not judged yet are asked about in the background, and their tabs are touched only if the answer is a distraction, so a study tab never reloads. If the app can't be reached, nothing is blocked. The bridge passes the page's `?at=` with every request.
- **Lockdown, site by site:** while Lockdown blocks, every whole-page visit is judged by its hostname ("youtube.com", without "www."). The app's own address and local addresses are never judged. A site not judged yet goes first to the blocked page, which shows only its name, pulsing, while the extension asks the app (`/api/site`, as the signed-in student) → Haiku 5.5, no thinking, low effort, one word in a fixed schema: study, distraction or unsure (`src/modules/extension/core/site.ts` and `judge.ts`; code keeps only those three words) → the verdict is kept in the extension for good, so each site is asked about once (about 1–2 seconds, a fraction of a cent). Study and unsure sites go on to the page; a distraction goes to focus. Known distractions are redirected before they load (a declarativeNetRequest redirect rule, which is why the extension asks for access to all sites). If the app can't be reached, or the student is signed out, or the model call fails, the site stays open for the rest of that Lockdown (nothing is kept).
- **The way through** (`blocked.html`): no focus screen of its own. A site not judged yet waits there briefly, showing only its name, pulsing, while Haiku answers; a known distraction passes through it on its way to the app's focus page. When Lockdown lifts while it's asking, the tab goes on to the site.
- **The popup** (toolbar button, `popup.html`): Status (Connected with the email, Signed out, or Can't reach Planner, from `/api/status`), Lockdown (Off when the setting is off, On when nothing is under way, Blocking until 4:44pm), the 24 hour bars learned from this browser (drawn like Hours in Options), and Open Planner.
- **Where the app is:** the extension learns the app's address from the app's own messages (default `http://localhost:3000`).
